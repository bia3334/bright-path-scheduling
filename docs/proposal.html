One screen that never double-books a lesson

A plan for replacing the front-desk spreadsheet with the smallest tool that takes the daily pain away. Written for the owner and the team first, with the technical detail tucked behind it.

Seed week 3 to 10 March 2026 "Today" in the demo Friday 6 March 2026, 10:00 Time box 2.5 hours of build

What is going wrong today

Everything runs from one shared spreadsheet and a set of WhatsApp groups. Mai, the receptionist, opens the sheet at seven, works out who teaches whom, and messages each tutor their day. When a family cancels she edits the sheet, messages the tutor, and tries to remember to tell the next family a slot is free. She is the only person who understands the sheet, and she goes on leave in eight weeks.

Owner

"Twice last term we had a student booked into two places at once. That can never happen again. If the system allows it, the system is broken."

Receptionist

"In exam season I put two students with one tutor in the same room, on purpose. It is half price and the families like it."

Tutor

"I get my day in a message, then a correction, then sometimes a third message. I do not always know which one is real."

Questions we would ask the owner first

The brief contradicts itself in a few places. Each answer below changes what gets built, so we have said what we assumed.

QuestionWhat we assumed for now
Is the exam pair a real product, or a rule Mai bends?A real product. The tool knows what a pair is and allows exactly that, nothing looser.
When a tutor cancels inside four hours, is the family charged?No. The rule is about who cancelled, so the tool records that.
Does a Monday lesson ever happen with your blessing?Never. The tool refuses it outright.
Does the six-a-day limit count late cancellations, where the tutor is still paid?No. Only lessons that actually happen count.
Should the tool send the WhatsApp messages?Not yet. Mai keeps sending them; the tool's job is to make sure what she sends is right.
The brief says 12 tutors and 6 rooms, the export shows 3 of each. Which is true?Both. The export is a slice. Rooms and tutors are lists you can add to, not fixed.

What the tool needs, and what we build first

Six things this tool will eventually need. In two and a half hours only one can be built properly, so we pick the one the owner called a dealbreaker and say plainly what waits.

FeatureIn plain words
firstConflict-safe bookingThe tool refuses any booking that puts a student, tutor or room in two places, gives a tutor a seventh lesson, or lands on a Monday. It tells you why. Exam pairs are allowed by name.
firstSee todayOpen the laptop and see the day as a grid like the one above. Click an empty cell to book. This is the screen the booking rule lives on, not a separate feature.
laterCancellationsMark a lesson cancelled, record who cancelled and whether it is charged under the four-hour rule.
laterWhat changed after 16:00A list of every move or cancellation since the tutors were told, so Mai knows exactly what to send.
laterTutor's daily messageThe text Mai pastes into WhatsApp, generated from the final schedule.
laterFamily waitlistWhen a slot frees up, show which family asked for that time.
Why this one. It is the only thing the owner said must never happen again, it happened three different ways last week, and it is the foundation the other five sit on. Cancellations and the 16:00 list are things you do to a booking. First there has to be a booking that cannot be wrong.

What stays broken for now. Cancelling still means changing a status by hand, and nobody is told automatically. Tutors still get their day from Mai on WhatsApp. The tool remembers every change it makes so the "what changed" list can be built next, but there is no screen for it yet.

How it fits together

Three pieces. A screen in the browser, a small service behind it, and a database that holds the bookings and refuses the bad ones. Nothing is installed on anyone's phone. The export is loaded once and the spreadsheet is retired.

What happens when Mai books a lesson

1Mai clicks an empty cell on today's grid: a room and a time.
2She types the student, picks the tutor and the length. Ticks "exam pair" if it is one.
3The tool checks the tutor's count for the day and asks the database to save.
4The database refuses if the room, tutor or student is already taken, or it is Monday.
5Either the lesson appears in the cell, or the cell shows the reason it was refused.

Where each rule is enforced

Some rules are simple enough that the database itself refuses to store a bad row. That is the strongest kind of protection: it holds even if someone bypasses the screen. Rules that need judgement live in the service, where the reasoning can be read.

RuleEnforced byWhy there
A room holds one lesson at a timedatabaseCannot be bypassed, works under two people booking at once.
A tutor is in one room at a timedatabaseSame.
A student is in one place at a timedatabaseThe owner's dealbreaker. Must survive any client.
Closed on Monday; lessons are 60 or 90 minutesdatabaseOne line each, no judgement needed.
No tutor takes more than six in a dayserviceNeeds to count, and to know that a pair counts once. That is a business reading, so it lives where people can read it.
An exam pair is two students, same tutor, room and timeserviceSame.

What the import will say

The export is loaded through the same rules as a live booking. Where two rows conflict, the first one in the file wins and the second is refused with a reason. We expect exactly these four refusals, matching the red cells above:

  • L008 Le Minh Chau, Wed 09:00: student already booked in R3.
  • L027 Bui An Nhien, Fri 20:30: Ngoc Anh already has six lessons that day.
  • L032 Vu Ha My, Mon 10:00: the centre is closed on Monday.
  • L034 Tran Bao Long, Tue 09:00: Ngoc Anh already teaching in R1.

The two cancelled lessons load as cancelled and take up no room or tutor time. The Wednesday exam pair loads as one pair.

For the technical reader

Stack. Java 17 with Spring Boot 3 and Spring JDBC, Flyway migrations, Postgres 16 in Docker with the btree_gist extension, React 18 with Vite for the single screen.

Data model. Tables for tutors, rooms, lessons and an append-only lesson_events log. A lesson has a generated time-range column; the three overlap rules are EXCLUDE constraints on that range keyed by room, tutor and student, ignoring cancelled rows and rows that share a pair id. Closed Monday is a CHECK on the day of week. A booking changed after the tutor was told is an event row whose timestamp is later than 16:00 the day before the lesson.

API. GET a day for the grid, POST a lesson which returns 409 with a list of reasons when refused, GET tutors for the form. Cancel, move and "what changed" endpoints are designed but not built.

Rejected endpoint. DELETE a lesson. A booking the tutor was told about cannot disappear. Cancellation is a state and an event, never a removal.

What you will be able to do at the demo

  • Open the tool and see Friday 6 March as a grid. No scrolling.
  • Try to book Le Minh Chau into a second room at 09:00 and watch it refuse, with the reason in the cell.
  • Book an exam pair and watch it accept.
  • Try to give Ngoc Anh a seventh lesson and watch it refuse.
  • Read the import log: four rows refused, each with its reason.

Bright Path is a composite of real clients and the data is synthetic. Names in the timetable are from the anonymised export.
